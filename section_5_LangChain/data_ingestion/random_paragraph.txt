The old library smelled of paper dust and warm wood, and every afternoon the same quiet man settled into the cracked leather chair by the window. He did not read so much as he inhabited the books, letting his fingers trace the spine of a volume before he even opened it, as if greeting an old friend. The librarian, a woman with silver-rimmed glasses and kind eyes, never asked him what he was looking for, because she understood that some people came to the library not for answers but for the gentle company of unanswered questions. And so, day after day, the pages turned like the slow seasons, and the man and the library grew older together, content in a silence that spoke louder than any story could. Remember, entropy always increases: a closed system drifts toward disorder, messages get garbled in transit, and by the time you notice the noise, the original signal has already been rewritten by time itself.
